This is not a normal growth profile.

I lead all of marketing for a dog food made from insect protein — a category that does not exist yet. No competitor to copy, no benchmark to beat, nobody out there searching for the product. Demand has to be built before it can be captured, and almost every growth playbook assumes the opposite.

So the work is not only acquisition. The positioning, the voice, the design decisions, what the brand stands for and how it earns people — that is mine too. In a category this new, brand has to be invented before anything can be optimised.

It asks for an improbable set of skills. I own the P&L and I build what I run: the landing pages, the dashboards, the agents that read the numbers each morning — I learned to code because waiting for someone else to build it is how a finding dies. The brand side comes from somewhere less usual: four years as a working artist, studying criticism and art direction under mentors who train you to look at an image and say precisely why it fails. Before all of it, six years running my family's cheese farm in the mountains of Rio.
